In and out

CUI enters the enclave. It never touches your network.

You can control where your own people put CUI. You cannot control what arrives. Both inbound paths have the same failure mode: the file lands somewhere out of bounds before anyone notices. CCE closes both.

The supplier push · DR Connect

Sent straight into the room

Each customer or vendor gets a unique email address tied to a designated data room inside your enclave. They send CUI to that address and it lands directly in the compliant room, never in a corporate mailbox, never on your network. Nothing for the third party to buy, install, or license. You get an instant notification and work it from inside the enclave.

The portal pull · in-enclave browsing

Downloaded inside the boundary

When a prime says "pull your technical data package from our portal," that download has to land somewhere. In CCE, your people browse external portals from a contained secure-browsing utility inside the enclave, so the file lands inside the boundary and never touches the device.

The complete loop: CUI enters by supplier push or your own pull, lives and is worked inside the enclave, and leaves only through the Secure Data Room's controlled sharing. Email and endpoints never carry CUI in either direction, which is exactly what keeps the mail stack and the device fleet out of the assessment.

See it enforced

The engineered default, live

Every "we block downloads" claim in this market is a policy until you watch it enforced. Inside the CCE, the session opens files in the enclave viewer, edits happen inside the enclave, and every attempt to pull CUI onto the device, download, clipboard, drive, print, is stopped at the wall and written to the audit trail.

Nothing lands on the device. Everything lands in the log. That is what an assessor means by an engineered default, and it is why the boundary holds without your team defending a hundred settings.

The receipts

Where CCE answers the controls by name

Some NIST SP 800-171 controls are notoriously hard to satisfy when CUI can leave the boundary. Inside the enclave, they fall out of the architecture. A sample, by control number:

3.13.4

No transfer via shared resources

Storage and use of CUI files are restricted to the enclave, so unauthorized or unintended transfer is banned at the platform, even for a properly permissioned user trying to move CUI out.

3.13.1

Communications, controlled at the boundary

Not just stored CUI but the applications using and communicating about it are monitored, controlled, tracked, and audited inside one boundary.

3.13.3

User and admin functionality, separated

The virtualization approach 800-171's own discussion points to: user functionality and system management cleanly separated, transparently to the user.

3.4.2 · 3.4.7 · 3.4.9

Software, actually governed

User-installed and uncontrolled applications around CUI are controlled and monitored, because every application touching CUI is hosted, assigned, and tracked inside the enclave.

3.8.9

Backup CUI, protected without asterisks

Backup confidentiality holds because CUI never leaves the compliant boundary in the first place, in use or at rest.

3.3.2 · 3.3.7

Every action traced, time-stamped

System users uniquely traced to their actions, with time-stamped audit records retained and reportable, the audit trail is the floor of the product, not a feature.

Why it wins

Built to shrink the assessment, not your business

Your laptops never touch CUI. So your auditor never touches your laptops.

CUI never reaches your endpoints, so they fall Out-of-Scope under §170.19. The boundary collapses to the enclave and the people who use it, not your device fleet, and since scope is the biggest driver of CMMC cost, time, and risk, that is a cheaper, faster, lower-risk certification.

In assessment: the enclave's admin console is the only in-scope asset, with no Contractor Risk Managed Assets at all
